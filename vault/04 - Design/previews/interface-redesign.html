Horizon Quests: interface redesign

Three directions for everything around the map. The node frames, the lines between quests and the marks (check, question mark, "!", padlock) are the real textures of the mod and do not change. Each direction shows the same quest twice: as a player sees it, and in edit mode. Text stays at the game's single font size, as the engine draws it; item icons are coloured stand-ins.

1Glass: a side drawer

The quest opens in a drawer on the right, so the map is never covered. Every task is a row with its name, a progress bar and a count; the state gives the drawer its colour; "Needs" and "Opens" are clickable rows at the bottom. Chapters are a rail of icons that widens on hover. In edit mode the same drawer becomes the editor: a task opens in place, nothing floats beside.

2Parchment: an open book

The Quest Book item opens a real book: tasks on the left page, the story and rewards on the right, a ribbon for the state, a wax seal to claim, page corners to go to what the quest needs or opens. Chapters are bookmarks. In edit mode the text is written in pencil and settings come on a sticky note.

3Vanilla+: Minecraft's own language

Looks like it shipped with the game: a framed window like the advancements screen, chapters as tabs on top, grey bevelled panels, item slots and buttons every player already knows. The least surprising, the easiest to read at any GUI scale.
